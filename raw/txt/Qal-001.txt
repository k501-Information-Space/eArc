Quantum Archivator Language (QAL)

Chapter 1 — Scope and Intent

Quantum Archivator Language (QAL) is a formal, deterministic archival language designed for long-term, append-only preservation of structured knowledge under strict audit and reproducibility constraints.

QAL is not a programming language, not a query language, and not a semantic model.
Its sole purpose is to record the existence of information artifacts in a mathematically verifiable manner, independent of interpretation, execution, or meaning.

The language is designed to:
	•	Preserve information as it appeared, not as it was understood.
	•	Separate presence from truth.
	•	Ensure temporal anchoring and cryptographic integrity.
	•	Remain readable, portable, and implementable across decades.

QAL operates under the principle of minimal expressiveness with maximal stability.

⸻

Design Constraints

QAL is constrained by the following non-negotiable rules:
	1.	Append-only — no mutation, deletion, or overwrite.
	2.	Deterministic serialization — identical input produces identical hashes.
	3.	Time anchoring — all structured artifacts are bound to UTC and Unix Epoch or explicitly marked UNKNOWN.
	4.	Interpretation-free — no semantic claims are encoded at the language level.
	5.	Layer separation — object integrity is independent of aggregation or packaging.

These constraints are intentional and foundational.
