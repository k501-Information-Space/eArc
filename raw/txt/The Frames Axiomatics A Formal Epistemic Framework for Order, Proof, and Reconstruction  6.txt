Abstract

This paper introduces the Frames Axiomatics, a formal epistemic framework designed to describe how knowledge, truth, and meaning can be represented, preserved, and reconstructed without semantic mutation. The framework is motivated by a core problem shared across modern science, computation, and information systems: how to maintain long-term stability, reproducibility, and auditability of knowledge while allowing indefinite growth, multiple perspectives, and evolving interpretations.

The Frames Axiomatics proposes a strict separation between structure, truth, and meaning. At its foundation lies the concept of the Frame, an atomic, append-only unit of reference that exists independently of observers and interpretations. Frames do not contain meaning and do not assert truth. Instead, they provide structured traces—ordered, time-anchored, and immutable—from which meaning can later be reconstructed by a reader.

Truth within the system is governed by a tri-state epistemic discipline: TRUE, FALSE, and UNKNOWN. UNKNOWN is the default and stable state; TRUE and FALSE may only be assigned through explicit proof artifacts, which themselves are represented as Frames. This proof-first approach prevents implicit truth claims, retroactive correction, and semantic drift. Importantly, the framework treats proof not as an external authority but as an internal, auditable object within the same reference space as all other information.

To ensure formal consistency and scalability, each Frame carries a compact Quantum Header (QH56), a fixed-size structural grammar that encodes states, guards, and relations without embedding content or semantics. The Quantum Header defines the possibility space of Frames but never their meaning. This distinction allows the system to remain stable over time while accommodating arbitrarily large and complex reference structures.

Meaning arises exclusively in the Archivator, a deterministic read function that projects, orders, and indexes Frames without modifying them. Multiple Archivators and read models may coexist, enabling parallel perspectives over the same immutable archive. This multiplicity does not introduce relativism, as the underlying order and truth states remain invariant.

To support large-scale organization, the framework introduces Blocks, Releases, and Quantumblocks—higher-order structural constructs that bundle Frames without copying or rewriting them. These constructs enable stable publication boundaries, long-term citation, and efficient navigation across vast reference spaces while preserving append-only integrity. Indexes and projections further enhance navigability but are explicitly defined as non-semantic and non-truth-bearing.

Communication within the Frames Axiomatics is formalized as a structural operation. Frames may be emitted, received, and observed across systems, but no transmission implies execution, command, or semantic effect. This eliminates classes of errors common in distributed systems, including implicit actions and hidden state changes.

A central contribution of this work is the explicit formalization of silence, non-knowledge, and abort conditions as valid epistemic states. By legitimizing non-assertion where evidence is absent, the framework aligns with established scientific practices such as null results and inconclusive analyses.

The framework culminates in a notion of canonical completeness: a system is considered complete not when it explains everything, but when it can cease extending its foundational assumptions without loss of validity. Once the axiomatic core is fixed, all further development becomes optional, additive, and non-disruptive.

The Frames Axiomatics is not a physical theory, a semantic ontology, or a domain-specific model. It is an epistemic infrastructure designed to support reproducible knowledge, long-term archives, and machine- and human-readable truth discipline across real and abstract event spaces.

Formal Model / Notation (Summary)

Let F be the set of Frames, H the set of Quantum Headers (QH56), P the set of Payloads, T a totally ordered set of time anchors, and G = {TRUE, FALSE, UNKNOWN} the epistemic state set.

A Frame is defined as:
F := (H, P, T, R)

Truth assignment is monotonic:
UNKNOWN → TRUE or FALSE only via explicit Proof Frames.

Meaning is not stored in Frames but arises through read-only Archivators:
A : F → V

Append-only ordering is the sole evolution rule.

Related Work (Summary)

The Frames Axiomatics generalizes scientific-grade practices found in large experimental infrastructures, provenance systems, and append-only archives. Unlike semantic or ontological systems, it avoids embedding meaning, prioritizing long-term stability and reconstructability over immediate inference.

────────────────────────────────────────────

Discussion & Implications

The framework provides epistemic stability rather than explanatory power. It enables durable archives where truth claims are constrained, interpretation remains external, and silence is a valid scientific outcome. Its primary contribution is a formal discipline for preserving order across time, scale, and evolving interpretation.
